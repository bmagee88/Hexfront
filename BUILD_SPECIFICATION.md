## B. Canonical physical-board visualization and state model

A **territory** is a fixed map base, not the same object as a numbered hex tile. Use a contiguous **37-territory axial-coordinate hex map** with edge adjacency, six regions (one central seven-hex region and five surrounding regions as a suggested map configuration; validate actual region assignments). Avoid assuming corner-touching means adjacency.

Every territory base always displays **exactly THREE rebel peg holes** in a rim OUTSIDE the stack area. **One colored rebel peg = exactly one rebel. Never use a numbered peg or one peg representing multiple rebels.** At most three pegs of any mix of player colors per territory. Peg holes must remain visible when numbered tiles are stacked or rearranged. There can be separate small blockade/ceasefire markers.

Each **numbered hex tile** has immutable: unique ID, combat value **1–6**, one of five printed resource-border colors plus text/icon (**Lumber, Iron, Food, Stone, Cattle**), deck/card kind (basic or special), and any printed ability. It has a flat recessed ownership depression with a **flush-fitting removable player-colored owner chip**; the stack must appear flat. UI should show an owner chip plus a side notch/stack-layer ownership strip so a buried foreign tile stays identifiable. A tile drawn from a shared deck is unowned until deployed; a purchased card belongs in the purchaser's private hand but receives the owner chip on deployment. Discarded tiles clear board ownership before reuse.

Each territory stores ordered tile IDs bottom-to-top, three explicit rebel slots (one peg/player identity per occupied slot), temporary blockade/effect markers, and region/coordinate. **Territory controller = owner of exposed top tile**. If removing an upper tile exposes a different player's tile (including a former ally), control instantly transfers to that owner. Neutral initial tiles are unowned. Never represent resource stocks as pegs on the map: resource stocks belong to player mats. Stack limit **3 numbered hexes**, distinct from exactly 3 rebel slots. Each buried tile gives **+1 defense**, maximum +2, regardless of buried number. Only exposed top tiles produce resources and activate persistent powers.

Basic tile deck: **120** tiles, four copies of each combination of strength 1–6 and the five resource types. Special cards: three distinct face-down decks, **Infrastructure, Weapons Tech, Diplomacy**, initially 15 cards each (three copies of five baseline abilities). Each special tile also needs a number and resource border; generate balanced fixed distributions. Keep all IDs/owner/state serializable. Explicitly distinguish board-tile ownership, territory control, rebel ownership, special-hand ownership, alliance membership, and the host role.

## C. Setup, snake opening, and turn sequence

1. Shuffle and deal one face-up, unowned basic hex onto each of 37 neutral territory bases.
2. Players choose three starting territories each in a **snake draft**: with four players A-B-C-D / D-C-B-A / A-B-C-D (generalize for 2–4). Choosing an unclaimed neutral space applies that player's ownership chip to its existing tile. After the first choice, require new choices adjacent to an owned territory when possible. Validate draft order and show legal selections.
3. Deal five basic hexes privately to each player. Starting resources **2 Food + 1 Lumber**, no alliances or rebels. Pick first turn order (host seed/shuffle, visible).
4. Create a global public **five-resource production forecast queue** from a bag containing one of each resource. The five initial displayed items come from one shuffled bag. Every round activate the leftmost resource; after the round, shift left, append a new bag draw. Refill/reshuffle the five-token bag only after exhausted; **one of each resource per five appended tokens**, not independent replacement rolls. Players can plan around the forecast.
5. At **start of each full round**, ALL players simultaneously receive one resource per territory they control whose exposed tile matches the active type, subject to **3 total production per player per wave** by default (player selects which eligible tiles if relevant to bonus production). Neutral tiles produce nothing; buried tiles produce nothing. Resolve start-round powers.
6. Normal turn: **draw 3 basic hexes up to hand limit 7**; take **3 actions in any order**, repeating allowed when legal; cleanup/discard to limit; next player. If the basic deck empties, reshuffle discarded basic hexes. Reaction guards and certain effects happen out of turn. Show round, turn, actions remaining, pending reaction, target selection, preview of results, and action history.
7. At full round end: resolve expirations, recalculate prestige holders, Sovereignty, coalition threshold and scores, check two-round VP victory, then advance resource queue. Define a deterministic exact order in code and display it.

Normal actions: Attack; Reinforce own stack (top or bottom); add Allied Support (bottom only); Reposition an owned top tile from a 2+ stack to an adjacent friendly territory without exceeding stack cap; Reorder one's own tiles in a stack (foreign allied tile cannot be moved above its territory controller's own top during an allied reorder); Scout (draw up to 2 basics); Buy Special; Bank Trade; Allied Trade; propose Alliance; relevant play/activate special tile; Patrol; and eligible Rebel actions. Model action costs and legal target previews in one authoritative engine. Do not allow movement that leaves a territory empty unless an explicit special effect says otherwise.

## D. Resources and special-deck purchases

- Printed resource border is permanent; only exposed matching territories produce on their resource round. **Resource storage cap 12 total tokens/player**, unless Granary or other power extends it. Excess gains are lost. Bank trade action: **three matching tokens for one chosen token**; Market Charter (when exposed) improves own bank trade to **2:1**.
- Resource purchase is an action; draw **one RANDOM secret special tile** from chosen deck into player's private hand after payment. Prices: **Infrastructure 2 Lumber + 1 Stone + 1 Food; Weapons Tech 2 Iron + 1 Cattle + 1 Lumber; Diplomacy 1 Food + 1 Cattle + 1 Stone**. Special hands share the overall 7-tile limit unless a rule overrides it. Empty special decks may recycle their discard piles. Show deck sizes but not order.
- Special abilities have explicit triggers: exposed passive, played-for-attack, on capture/deployment, one-shot/discarded, or out-of-turn reaction. Persist temporary durations using server-tracked markers.

**Infrastructure deck baseline (3 copies each):**
1. Fortress — exposed +2 defense in its territory.
2. Lumber Mill — exposed, +1 Lumber on Lumber rounds, subject to production cap.
3. Granary — exposed, increase owner's resource storage cap by 3.
4. Workshop — exposed, increase owner's resource production cap by 1.
5. Border Fort — exposed, no rebel retreat INTO its territory.

**Weapons Tech deck baseline (3 copies each):**
1. Siege Engine — when played on attack and winning by at least 4, remove one additional defending layer if present; resolve capture atomically if last layer falls.
2. Ballista — when played against 2+ defending layers, +2 attack.
3. Raiders — when this card takes a territory, steal one available resource from former controller.
4. Cavalry — when played as attack, can target two edges away via exactly one intervening territory controlled by attacker (validate path).
5. Blockade Unit — exposed, blocks rebel retreat INTO its territory.

**Diplomacy deck baseline (3 copies each):**
1. Treaty — one-shot, discard as one participant's payment to establish a formal alliance.
2. Ceasefire — one-shot, protect an owned territory from attacks until the owner's next turn begins.
3. Envoy — deployment: claim an adjacent single-layer neutral territory by replacing its neutral hex with Envoy, no combat.
4. Shield Pact — reaction: when defending a formal ally, discard for +3 defense; occupies that battle's one guard slot.
5. Market Charter — exposed, bank trades cost only 2 matching resources for 1 chosen.

Keep card abilities in a typed data registry, with clear textual tooltip and server-side test. Card effects that are one-shot don't become permanent board tiles unless explicitly defined otherwise.

## E. Standard combat, stacks, and guard reactions

- From an owned territory, attack an adjacent neutral/enemy territory (not a current formal ally). Spend an action, choose one hex from your private hand.
- **Attack = source territory exposed top number + played hand hex number + modifiers**.
- **Defense = target exposed top number + number of buried layers (+0..2) + exposed specials + offered rebel defense + optional surprise guard + modifiers**.
- The defender may react by discarding **ONE** hand hex, adding its number to defense. If Shield Pact is used, it takes the same single guard slot; do not stack both. There is a host-configurable reaction timeout that defaults to pass; no autoplay before reaction is resolved. Neutral territories cannot guard.
- Attack must be STRICTLY GREATER; ties defend. On failure, attacker discards played hand tile; defender stack unchanged; used guard is discarded.
- On success, remove/discard target TOP tile. If defender layers remain, discard attack tile too and immediately update territory controller to newly exposed tile owner. If the final layer is gone, put attack tile in target with attacker's owner chip and take territory. The original source remains. Advanced Siege Engine might remove an extra layer.
- A battle against a 3-deep stack normally requires multiple successful attacks. No automatic permanent damage to lower tile numbers. Example: source 4 + hand 5 = 9 vs top 6 + 2 buried = 8; attack succeeds but only top 6 is removed; hand 5 discarded. Example: source 4 + hand 3 = 7 vs top 5 + 1 buried = 6; enemy guards with a 2, defense 8, attack fails; both hand tiles discarded.
- Optional ally ownership interaction: while an alliance is still valid, removing Red top tile from Red 5 / Blue 2 exposes Blue 2 and Blue gains the territory immediately, without the attacker taking it.

## F. Formal alliances, Sovereignty, betrayal, and Coalition Threat

- Formal alliance requires consent from **both** players, who each independently pay **1 Food + 1 Cattle + 1 Stone OR one Treaty tile**. Initiator spends one action. At most **one formal ally per player**. Use proposal/accept/decline with timeouts and server verification. Two-player games can disable formal alliances/Coalition Threat as the tabletop playtest default.
- Perks: place own numbered support hex at **BOTTOM** of ally's territory stack, only if under stack cap and at most **ONE foreign-owned supporting hex per territory**; resource transfers of **max 2 tokens total per alliance per round**, paid with an action; Shield Pact and other formal shared-defense effects; coordinate blockades and rebel containment. Allies cannot attack each other while allied, combine two source-hex attack values, merge territory ownership, share prestige titles, or win jointly. Each player retains individual victory conditions.
- **Ending alliance is permitted on the initiating player's own turn without spending an action.** Mutual separation (both consent) removes all partnership foreign support tiles, discards them, produces NO rebels, NO VP penalty. Unilateral separation removes all foreign support tiles from BOTH former partners' stacks. Each removed support tile produces **one rebel peg for its original owner** in its former territory (or nearest legal adjacent hole; if full, a dormant **Rebel Reserve**, used for CREATION ONLY). **Apply a permanent -2 VP penalty to the unilateral initiator ONCE for the entire breakup IF AND ONLY IF at least one rebel is created**. With no support tiles creating rebels, no penalty. Count reserve creations as created. Never apply a betrayal penalty merely because an alliance ends. Both sides get +2 Sovereignty immediately after separating if eligible, but permanent penalties persist.
- Former allies may not form a new formal alliance with each other for **three complete rounds**. Server tracks cooldowns, past betrayal counts, and reserve pegs.
- **Sovereignty:** EACH player with >=1 territory and NO current formal ally gets **+2 CURRENT/conditional VP**. It vanishes immediately when forming an alliance and returns after leaving if still landholding. It is NOT an exclusive title and doesn't build up over time. Landless rebels do not receive it.
- **Coalition Threat:** when two formally allied players together control **MORE THAN HALF** all map territories (on 37 spaces, at least 19), they are a Dominant Coalition. Every player OUTSIDE that alliance gains **+1 attack** when targeting either coalition member and steals **one additional available resource** when successfully raiding a coalition member (up to two in total). Recompute this condition whenever territory control or alliance status changes; it ends immediately below threshold or on dissolution. Show a prominent public warning and the count. The perk is based on actual dominance, NOT merely forming any alliance.
- Players can make informal deals or send resources to rebels as allowed, but ONLY formal alliances confer official shared-defense, bottom-stack support, and special ally transfers. No shared VP.

## G. Rebels, three holes, rebel economy, survival

- A rebel exists only as a **single player-colored peg in one of exactly three base holes**. **One peg = one rebel = +1 strength**. Up to three rebel pegs total in any territory across all colors; same-color pegs at same location form one moving group. The pegs are not stacked numbered tiles; do not alter underlying terrain/resource production or controller merely by existing.
- Rebels arise when alliance support converts on a unilateral breakup, or when a player loses their final territory: spawn one rebel in the territory just lost if possible, otherwise an adjacent legal hole or dormant Rebel Reserve for spawn overflow. **Rebel Reserve is never a retreat destination and cannot be used to escape containment.** Dormant spawn pegs deploy to a legal non-full territory at the start of owner's next turn if possible; cannot act while in reserve.
- Rebel Move: move the WHOLE same-owner group one edge to a neighboring base with enough empty holes for all pegs; can merge up to base limit. A group of 3 needs 3 free holes. A territory with 3 occupied holes is impassable for adding rebels (even if color matches), except explicit ability.
- Underground attack: from rebels ALREADY in the target (regardless of whether their owner has other land), spend one action and a hand hex. **Attack = played hex number + number of attacker's rebel pegs in target + modifiers**, NOT source territory top value. Defend with ordinary stack defense and one eligible guard. Win removes top; if layers remain discard played hex and rebels remain; if final layer removed, deploy attacking hex with player's owner chip, take territory, and **REMOVE ALL attacking player's rebel pegs in that captured territory**. Losing attack discards hand hex; rebels stay. Rebels attack from inside, not automatically from bottom: they remove TOP defending tile unless a special rule explicitly specifies otherwise. (This supersedes an early bottom-stack attack experiment.)
- Rebel defense/mercenaries: a rebel faction present in a territory may VOLUNTARILY add all of its pegs there (+1 each) to the controller's defense for a battle; at most ONE rebel faction may pledge to that battle. May accept negotiated resources for help; no automatic obligation. Rebel defense does not use the defender's one hand-guard slot. On later battles the rebels may choose differently.
- Rebel Raid: once per rebel group per turn, spend an action and steal **one available resource** from current territory controller's stock (a successful Coalition Threat raid steals an additional one if available). If no resources remain, steal zero. Players may voluntarily send resources to active rebels via legal Trade action. Recruit: spend **1 Food + 1 Cattle** and one action, add one same-color peg to an existing rebel group with a vacant hole (up to the territorial 3-hole limit).
- If a player controls **zero territories**, enter landless Rebel Mode: draw **2 basic hexes up to hand limit 7**, take **2 rebel actions** per turn (Move, Underground Attack, Raid, Recruit, Reorganize/discard one draw two, Buy Special, eligible powers). No normal resource production and no Sovereignty VP; can still use stored, raided, gifted resources and special decks. Return to normal as soon as a territory is recaptured. All rebels except the absolutely LAST rebel of a completely landless player are mortal.
- Suppression: territory controller spends one action and a hand hex against a specifically selected hostile rebel faction in that territory. **Suppression = controlling top hex number + spent hand hex number**. **Rebel resistance = pegs of targeted faction + one optional rebel defender's hand guard**, and strict > wins; ties favor rebel. On success discard suppressor's hand tile, REMOVE ONE rebel peg, then survivors of that targeted group retreat TOGETHER to an adjacent legal territory with enough open holes not blocked by applicable patrols/forts/units. If the group reaches zero it dies. On failure discard the tile and rebels remain. Suppression alone does not capture/change the territory. If no retreat is available, remaining group is captured and removed.
- **Last Rebel protection:** ONLY if its owner currently has ZERO territories and EXACTLY ONE rebel peg TOTAL (including any reserve peg), a successful suppression does not remove that peg; it MUST retreat instead. If there is NO LEGAL RETREAT, that peg is destroyed and the player is PERMANENTLY ELIMINATED. No teleportation, immortal reserve, or automatic next-turn reappearance after a blocked retreat. A player is eliminated when they have neither land nor any pegs anywhere including creation reserves. **One player remaining** may win by elimination (configurable, default on). Last-rebel protection never applies to a player who still owns land or has additional rebels.
- Legal retreat: adjacent traversable base with sufficient EMPTY peg holes for whole group and no applicable blockade. Retreat cannot go to a reserve and cannot pass through a full base. Patrol action (once per player per round): temporarily mark a controlled territory to prevent rebel entry/retreat; max two active patrols per player; expires at end of next turn. Exposed Border Fort/Blockade Unit block retreat into their bases. Encirclement requires positioning AND a successful suppression. Display potential retreat destinations and why unavailable, and expose escape routes clearly in UI; make the last-rebel sequence fair, not an opaque instant death.
- Optional rebel tactical response for playtesting: **Slip Through** (discard a hand hex of value >=4 to ignore ONE blockade when moving/retreating, but NEVER ignore the hard 3-hole capacity). Flag off by default until evaluated. Likewise optional **Amnesty** Diplomacy effect: remove up to two ordinary rebel pegs in controlled territory by peaceful action, paying the affected owner one Food; cannot bypass last-rebel mandatory retreat/containment. If implemented, make it an additional deck variant and document composition changes.

## H. Prestige titles, VP, and victory

Five public, exclusive, transferable prestige titles are each **2 current VP**:
1. **Longest Supply Line:** longest SIMPLE path of connected territories personally controlled (no repeat vertices); minimum 5. Don't count ally-owned hexes. Implement a correct bounded search with caching/pruning on 37-node graph; test branches/cycles.
2. **Strongest Army:** most deployed Weapons Tech tiles owned by a player anywhere on board, including buried; minimum 2.
3. **Master Builder:** most deployed Infrastructure tiles owned, including buried; minimum 2.
4. **Trade Dominance:** most DISTINCT resource-border types exposed across territories currently controlled; minimum 3.
5. **Diplomatic Influence:** most deployed Diplomacy tiles owned, including buried; minimum 2.

A title transfers only when a challenger STRICTLY exceeds the current valid holder; a tie retains an eligible current holder. If nobody qualifies or the current holder stops qualifying and multiple challengers tie, title remains vacant until a unique eligible leader emerges. Titles are never shared. Display holders and the exact progress metrics for all players.

Current score = **2 × held titles + 2 if currently Sovereign − 2 × cumulative qualifying unilateral betrayal events**. Negative scores allowed. No automatic points for each owned territory; territory count matters for production/Coalition Threat/tie-breaking. Winning is individual, even in an alliance.

**Victory claim:** At the end of a complete round, a player with at least **8 current VP** becomes a contender. If that same player still has >=8 at end of the FOLLOWING complete round, eligible contenders can win. Highest current VP among eligible contenders wins; tie-break by personally controlled territories, then fewest betrayals; if still tied play another round. If player falls below 8 the claim fails. Compute claims from end-of-round snapshots, not transient midturn score spikes. After full elimination of all opponents, remaining player wins if the optional elimination victory rule is enabled.

## L. Rule conflict policy / acceptance criteria

The specifications here are the canonical baseline; earlier brainstorm alternatives are variants only. Where a minor rule remains genuinely unspecified, choose the simplest consistent, non-exploitable interpretation, declare it in `RULE_DECISIONS.md` and surface it in the in-app rules; do not silently omit functionality. Keep actual values/config in a shared settings object rather than scattering magic numbers. Make normal rounds, reactive prompts, and end-of-round timing testable. Don't introduce fabricated API contracts.

**Done means:** two or more people on different browsers can create/join a room, ready up, snake-draft, draw and privately inspect hexes, obtain correct queued resource production, place and stack hexes with flush ownership chips, play real attacks/guards, purchase and use each deck, form and break alliances correctly, see Sovereignty/Coalition Threat affect gameplay, deploy and move 1-for-1 pegs in three sockets, raid/suppress/uprise, intentionally contain and eliminate a landless final rebel, earn/lose titles, and reach a correctly adjudicated winner — with persistent reconnection, tests, and working deployment documentation.
