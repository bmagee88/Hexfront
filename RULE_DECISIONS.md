# Explicit rule decisions

These decisions resolve underspecified details without changing the canonical baseline. The Field Manual exposes the gameplay decisions.

1. **Map:** Radius-three axial hex disk: 37 connected territories, edge adjacency only. The radius-one central region has seven bases. The remaining thirty are partitioned into five connected angular sectors. Region labels have no extra VP/economy effects.
2. **Timing:** Setup, private deal, initial forecast, round-one simultaneous production, first turn draw. Thereafter: finish active turn; when the order wraps, update prestige and Sovereignty-derived scores, record/check two-round claims, check a unique winner, increment round, shift forecast and append one bag draw, reset alliance transfers, distribute all production, deploy reserve pegs, expire start-turn Ceasefires/patrols, draw the active player's basics, set actions. Scores and coalition dominance update after every successful command as well as round end. No automatic points for territory count.
3. **Turn hand cap:** The three normal draws (two while landless) stop at the combined seven-card cap. Scout stops there too. A free manual discard can create space; a player may not draw hidden tiles before choosing a discard. No forced overdraw UI is needed.
4. **Resources:** All printed and bonus production in one wave is the same active resource, so automatic clipping at the production cap has no token-choice consequence. Stored tokens remain private-hand-independent public stocks. Gains above capacity are lost. Losing an exposed Granary immediately discards overflow in fixed order: Cattle, Stone, Food, Iron, Lumber. Bank trade gives a different resource. Resource transfers are 1–2 matching tokens per order; formal partners share a two-token-per-round allowance. Active rebels may receive transfers even without a formal alliance. Raiders' on-capture theft takes the first available resource in registry order. Rebel Raid uses the selected preferred type, then other available stock. Coalition extra theft applies to Rebel Raid.
5. **One-shots:** Treaty, Ceasefire, and Shield Pact cannot be deployed into stacks or used as attack/suppression tiles. Treaty substitutes one alliance contribution. Ceasefire is a dedicated own-turn one-shot on controlled territory. Shield Pact is an ally-only out-of-turn reaction and occupies the battle's only hand-guard slot. Other ordinary hand guards use their printed number.
6. **Powers:** Passive abilities operate only while exposed and benefit that territory controller. Ownership for special-deployment prestige counts every owned deployed special, including buried supports. Envoy has a dedicated deployment order that replaces an adjacent neutral single-layer tile; using it as a regular reinforcement or attack does not additionally claim another territory. Cavalry's intermediary must be personally controlled. Siege removes at most two defending layers; capture deploys the attack tile only when the stack empties. Fortress is +2 in addition to buried-layer defense.
7. **Consent and payment:** Both alliance contributions are paid atomically at acceptance. An initiator's offered Treaty/resources are not reserved; acceptance fails without partial payment if either side can no longer pay. A pending diplomatic offer does not freeze unrelated turns. Offers expire after sixty seconds. A mutual-separation request spends no action; the partner must accept. Unilateral separation needs no consent. The breakup cooldown permits reunion starting round `breakupRound + 4`, after three complete intervening rounds.
8. **Support inheritance:** Control is always derived from the exposed owner, never cached. Reorder is controller-only and keeps any foreign support at the bottom. Once a formerly buried support becomes its own owner's exposed territory, it is no longer a foreign support in that territory.
9. **Rebel creation:** First prefer the former base, then its adjacent bases in map order; only creation overflow enters reserve. A reserve deploys at turn start to the first base with a hole in public map order. Reserves never act, defend, raid, or provide retreat. A normal move follows the same blockade rules as retreat. Moving a raided group carries its raid marker to its destination, so merging/movement cannot reset its once-per-turn raid. Recruitment does not reset a raid.
10. **Rebel survival:** Successful suppression targets one faction. The optional guard belongs to that faction. Remove one peg except for the absolutely last landless peg; survivors must retreat as a group. Select the first adjacent unblocked base with enough holes. No such base means the survivors are captured, including a protected last peg. Escape destinations and blockades appear in the territory inspector. Landless turn state switches to normal eligibility upon recapture, but the player retains their current turn's remaining action allocation and does not gain an extra draw midturn.
11. **Rebel defense:** One nonattacking faction physically present may volunteer all its local pegs before the defender resolves their guard or the window expires. A hand guard resolves the battle immediately. Negotiate and arrange gifts before the defender confirms; there is no automatic payment or automatic rebel obligation. Rebel pledge does not use the guard slot.
12. **Patrol:** One action per commander per round, at most two active. It blocks all rebel entry and expires at the start of the owner's second subsequent turn, equivalent to through the end of their next turn.
13. **Prestige:** Longest Supply Line uses an exact simple-path search over at most 37 vertices, memoization, reachable-component, endpoint, and articulation bounds, plus board-ownership caching. No repeated vertices, ally land, approximate length, or hidden timeout fallback. Special titles count owned deployed tile kinds. Valid incumbents keep ties. Without a unique eligible leader and without a tied eligible incumbent, a title is vacant.
14. **Victory:** Claims come only from distinct complete-round-end snapshots. A score falling below eight at a later round end clears its claim. Tied eligible contenders compare score, personal territory count, then fewer qualifying betrayals; exact ties continue. There is no joint victory. The optional last-survivor rule is on by default. Zero land and zero active/reserve pegs permanently eliminates a commander into public spectator mode.
15. **Production transport:** Authoritative versioned HTTP polling is used instead of WebSockets so the entire app can deploy on Netlify. No static host is misrepresented as a persistent Socket.IO server. Timers and bots progress when a connected client polls. A paused battle's reaction window is reset to its configured duration on resume. Actions never auto-resolve before their reaction unless the target is neutral and no nonattacking rebels can react.
16. **Privacy and audit:** Host role grants management, never hand/deck access. Cryptographic server entropy prevents a visible host seed from predicting the deck. Entropy and the private command transcript stay server-side after the match too. Public exports contain only the chosen host seed and public action log. Authorized server operators can audit stored snapshots; the host browser has no private-audit privilege. Rematches use fresh server entropy.
17. **Optional playtests:** Blitz, Adjacent Support, Draft Reinforcements, factions, secret objectives, queue manipulation, Highway, Merchant Guild, Trade Agreement, Saboteur, Shieldbearers, Amnesty, and Slip Through are not implemented in this release. Their toggles are absent, and the UI explicitly states they are unavailable. Built-in chat and host rollback are also absent. These are disclosed scope boundaries, not active baseline rules.
